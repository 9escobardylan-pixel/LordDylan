Before you keep reading

Three reasons you should not hire me.

Most applications open by begging. I'd rather be straight with you. There are a few kinds of teams I'm genuinely bad for and I'd rather you learn that on line one than three weeks into a contract. So before the pitch: here's who should close this and move on.

  1. 01

    If creative has to clear five approvals before it goes live, skip me.

    My entire edge is speed: write, launch, read the data, cut what's dead, double down on what works, repeat. Bury that loop under sign-off chains and a forty-reply Slack thread and you've killed the one thing you hired me for. I'm not asking to go rogue. I'm asking for enough rope to test before the moment's gone.

  2. 02

    I'm a bad fit if you want your opinions repeated back to you.

    When an angle's going to bleed your budget, you'll hear it from me before the money's gone, direct, not difficult. And I won't put my name on creative for a product I wouldn't hand to a friend. If you need a quiet pair of hands to run with whatever the room decided, that's a real role. Just not one I'd do well.

  3. 03

    If a stack of agency titles is what makes you comfortable, I'll come up short.

    No decade of logos behind me, no "Senior" in front of my name. What you get instead is someone who shows up with ideas you didn't ask for and has already torn apart your current ads before our first call. This isn't a comfortable lateral move for me. It's the thing I'm out to prove. If you'd feel safer with someone resting on a résumé, hire them.

None of that scared you off? Good. Here's how I'd prove it without making you sit through an interview.

Give me 7 days and I'll out-run your best ad or it costs you nothing.

Be honest, you've probably waded through a pile of "hardworking team player, passionate about marketing" applications this week and couldn't name one an hour later. None of them sounded like a person. This one will, so stick with me for thirty seconds.

The deal is dead simple: hand me whatever ad is performing best for you right now, and I'll rebuild it three different ways using the exact process behind everything I make.

If my hooks don't beat your control in 7 days, I'll Venmo you $50 and never contact you again. Not because I can afford to lose $50. Because I can't afford to waste your time or mine.

Where do I get the nerve to promise that?

Because I'm not guessing. While most people throw spaghetti at the algorithm and pray, every ad I write moves through the same three stages:

  1. 01

    Listen first.

    I dig through the places your buyers speak freely: the one- and five-star reviews, Reddit threads, the comments your team scrolls past, and lift the exact phrases they use when nobody's pitching them. By the time I write a word, I already know what moves them.

  2. 02

    Engineer the hook.

    That real customer language goes into AI tooling I've dialed in specifically for ad writing, and out come hooks that land like they were pulled straight from your buyer's own head, not warmed-over lines half your competitors are already running.

  3. 03

    Build the stopper.

    The strongest hook becomes a finished static or AI-video ad that's almost annoying to scroll past, made from footage you already own, ready to launch the same day I hand it over.

Notice I'm not starting from scratch. I take the ad already pulling its weight and drop a stronger engine under the hood. A little unfair to whoever you're up against, but that's on them.
